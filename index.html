Emmanuel Ruiz is a software engineering manager. He leads the web platform behind a family of websites in a regulated industry, and X-Ray, the quality gate that audits them every week and on demand.

This site is where he writes about building software at the frontier: working with AI agents as a team, shipping at a pace that used to be unthinkable, and refusing to let quality be the price of that pace.

The essays are long, written for engineers and the people who lead them, and they favor evidence over opinion. Each one records what was built, what was measured, what went wrong, and what the next person can reuse. X-Ray, which many of them mention, crawls a website with a real browser and reports the content, link, rendering, accessibility and answer-engine problems that should stop a page from going live. It was built in a month with AI agents as part of the team, and that month is the subject of the first essay.

Everything here is the author's own view, not his employer's. Quote freely, with a link. The full text of every essay is also published as plain Markdown for people and machines that prefer it that way: see llms.txt.
